# CH101 separate sleeve-end authoring — 2026-09-18

## What was actually made

A new hollow sleeve-end mesh, separate from the fused source body, now exists in
a saved Blender scene. It has 512 vertices / 1,024 triangles, eight axial profiles,
32 angular samples, outer/inner walls and annular end rims. The graphite fabric,
gold trim direction and exposed hand relationship draw on the pinned approved
character sheet. Two narrow material lanes use the sleeve's own faces, not floating
decals. Small geometric folds are capped at 0.7 mm amplitude within the sleeve span.

This is an **early shape/design hypothesis**, not detailed finished clothing or
an image-to-3D reconstruction that exactly matches the illustration. Inspection
still shows a fairly regular, faceted tube and simplified trim; cloth folds,
seams, proportions and finish need further authoring. No full-character score
or visual-quality pass is assigned.

The 96 mm span (2 to 98 mm above the authored wrist), ellipse radii, gold-lane
positions and 1.5 mm radial wall offset are authored estimates, not measurements
recovered from orthographic art. Placement uses the authored hand frame, not a
claim that the previously rejected orange plane is the real sleeve/skin seam.

The 2 mm nominal axial separation is deliberately visible; it is not a welded
connection, normal-thickness guarantee or an exact continuous minimum clearance.
The upper opening is also not attached. The intersection with the original body
remains explicit: it cannot be called integration just because the isolated
parts do not cross. Surface checks do not prove solid containment or clearance
during animation. The separate self-surface test is recorded alongside the
generic topology audit, which does not itself perform that test.

An automatic study UV unwrap exists. Two added study materials have not been
folded into the final character material budget or atlas. No Armature binding,
weights, deformation tests, FBX package or Unity import was performed.

All pre-existing mesh geometry/material digests and the input file SHA256 are
unchanged. Visibility is configured only for the review views. No source was
cut, welded or removed; prior trial objects and the dense cuff remain preserved.

## Next implementation step

1. Establish explicit distal/proximal boundary rings on working copies of the
   sleeve and reduced cuff. Their different tessellation and current separation
   mean proximity is not a valid weld rule.
2. Build and validate a reversible sleeve-to-cuff transition on those *authored
   parts*, preserving originals. Keep hand/equipment crossings at zero and reject
   flipped or self-crossing transition faces. Do not cut the original body.
3. Refine the authored sleeve silhouette and folds against the approved art and
   fit the upper opening. Only after an explicit replacement region is justified
   should source-body replacement/deformation be tested on a working copy.

These tasks do not remove the remaining face/hair/outfit and full-character
quality work or the separate human Gate B requirement.
